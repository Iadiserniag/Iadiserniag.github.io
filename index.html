About Me

I am a PhD student in Data Science at Sapienza University of Rome. My research focuses on assessing the trustworthiness and explainability of Large Reasoning Models, particularly for applications in economics and finance. I am a member of the RSTLess research group and a research intern at Banca d'Italia.

Short Bio

I graduated summa cum laude from LUISS Guido Carli University with a BSc in Management and Computer Science and from Sapienza University of Rome with an MSc in Data Science. During my studies I developed a strong interest in the intersection between data science and economics.

During both my bachelor's and master's studies, I collaborated with the Applied Research Team (ART) at the Directorate General for Information Technology of Banca d'Italia. My bachelor's internship project focused on the structural properties of the Bitcoin Lightning Network using graph theory. I later returned for my master's thesis internship, where I worked on authorship and topic analysis in Italian central bank communication using Natural Language Processing methods.

In 2025 I joined again ART as a research intern working on Large Language Models and their applications in macroeconomic forecasting. This work led to the publication Prompting for Policy: Forecasting Macroeconomic Scenarios with Synthetic LLM Personas, presented at ICAIF 2025.

I am currently a research intern at the Directorate General for Economics, Statistics and Research of Banca d'Italia, where I develop NLP-based methods to analyze central bank communication and build an index of perceived monetary policy stance. Part of this work is available through the Central Bank Textual Tone Index project. This internship follows my selection as "particularly deserving" in the Banca d'Italia Giorgio Mortara scholarship.
